# Why LabNote?

CapstanAI LabNote is not trying to replace every way you already keep work.
It is a small, visible layer for the parts of AI-assisted work that need to
survive a chat: a task, a source, a decision, a critique, a handoff, a review,
or the next action.

It can sit beside a local folder, Git, cloud storage, a project-management
tool, a research library, a context file, or a platform project area. The
question is not “Which one wins?” The question is whether the project has a
clear, durable route for the next human or AI session.

## The problem LabNote solves

Most people already improvise some form of continuity. They paste summaries
between chats, keep notes in a separate file, bookmark old conversations,
write handover sheets, or try to remember which AI was told what.

That can work perfectly well for a short task. It becomes fragile when work
crosses sessions, tools, people, days, or months. The record becomes scattered,
the next session must be re-briefed, and the human becomes the only person who
knows where the thread went.

LabNote turns that informal practice into a reusable project habit. It gives
each session a known way in, a limited route through the relevant work, places
to leave useful output, and points where a human decision is needed.

## It is not automatic model memory

A long context window can help a model read more in one sitting. It does not,
by itself, decide what should remain important after the session, show the next
tool why a decision was made, or create a selective record that a human can
inspect.

LabNote does not enlarge a model’s native memory or silently capture your
conversations. A human or an AI session deliberately writes the record. That is
why the trail can be checked, corrected, reviewed, and carried to another
tool.

Context length is useful. Continuity is a separate job.

## It is not a replacement for context files

Files such as `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md` are useful ways to tell an AI about a
repository: where important files are, how to run tests, and what local rules
apply.

LabNote complements them. Its job is to route ongoing project work: where a
session begins, what it should read, where it may leave work, how that work is
reviewed, and when the session should stop and ask.

A context file can tell an AI what kind of repository it is in. LabNote gives
it a route through the work happening there.

## Why not just make a folder?

You can. For a short project, a folder, a to-do list, and a few well-written
notes may be enough.

LabNote earns its place when you want that simple practice to remain useful as
the project grows. It is the version you do not have to keep redesigning:
ordinary files plus repeatable routes for handoffs, review, provenance, and
the next action.

You do not have to use every part of it. A LabNote can start as a small task
list or a single document trail. It can grow into a fuller working library and
audit record when the work genuinely needs that depth.

## Why it deliberately stays small

LabNote does not try to save everything as it happens. Not every message is a
durable project record. The aim is to keep the material that will help later:
the source, decision, critique, handoff, review, and next action.

Selective persistence reduces noise and can avoid needless rereading,
resummarising, and context loading. It also keeps the record easier for a human
to inspect.

The structured routes do not make a model smarter or infallible. They narrow
routine coordination work: a known entrance, limited reading routes, clear
write targets, and defined places to stop and ask. That makes the project less
dependent on a session improvising its own filing system.

No shared-memory theatre.

That is not a sneer at model memory, RAG, or other persistence systems. It is a
design preference for visible files, bounded behaviour, and claims a user can
inspect for themselves.

## Human control and recovery

LabNote is one part of your stack. You decide what belongs in it, what requires
review, how much detail the project needs, and what happens next.

If a chat ends, a model changes, a tool disappears, or a project goes quiet,
the visible record can give a later session a way back into the work. Keep the
workspace under normal version control and backup: LabNote preserves the
meaningful trail; version control preserves history; backups preserve
availability.

LabNote is not disaster recovery. It is a way to make a project more legible
when recovery or rebuilding is necessary.

## Use the smallest useful amount

If a one-shot answer is enough, use the best tool available and get on with it.
If a plain folder is enough, use a plain folder.

Use LabNote when you want a visible working trail that can grow with the
project without becoming a hidden service or a second brain.
